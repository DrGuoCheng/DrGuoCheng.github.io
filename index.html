👋 I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi, where I direct the FAST Vision Lab. Before joining TAMU-CC, I worked as a software engineer at Bloomberg L.P., New York.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Purdue University in 2022, advised by Professor Jiangyu Zheng. My research develops efficient and trustworthy perception for autonomous and intelligent systems, spanning computer vision, deep learning, multimodal AI, and autonomous driving. I focus on processing streaming visual and multimodal data under practical constraints on computation, memory, latency, and reliability. My work has appeared in IEEE T-ITS, IEEE T-IV, ICPR, IEEE IV, and ITSC.

RESEARCH — I develop methods for processing streaming visual and multimodal data efficiently and reliably, with applications to autonomous vehicles, robotics, and edge intelligence. Current directions include:
  • Sequential and streaming visual perception
  • Efficient, lightweight, and deployable AI
  • Trustworthy visual and multimodal intelligence
  • Autonomous vehicles, robotics, and edge systems

Preprint · 2026

Semantic Misalignment in Vision-Language Models under Perceptual Degradation

Guo Cheng

arXiv preprint, 2026

PaperPDF
Abstract

Vision-language models are increasingly used in autonomous driving and embodied AI, but their behavior under realistic visual-perception degradation remains insufficiently understood. This work studies semantic misalignment using controlled degradation of semantic segmentation on Cityscapes. It introduces language-level measures of hallucination, critical omission, and safety misinterpretation, revealing a disconnect between pixel-level robustness and downstream multimodal reliability.
