About Me

I am a tenure-track Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi, where I am building a research group focused on efficient and trustworthy perception for autonomous systems.

My research develops methods that enable intelligent systems to process streaming visual and multimodal data under practical constraints on computation, memory, latency, and reliability. A central direction of my work is Sequential Semantic Segmentation (SE3), an inference paradigm for eliminating redundant computation across spatial and temporal inputs while preserving prediction quality. I also study the reliability of vision-language models under degraded and uncertain visual conditions.

Before joining TAMU-CC, I worked as a software engineer at Bloomberg L.P. I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Purdue University in 2022, advised by Professor Jiangyu Zheng. My work has appeared in IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles, ICPR, IEEE IV, and ITSC.

Research Group

I am building a research group at TAMU-CC centered on efficient and trustworthy perception for autonomous systems. Research

Efficient Sequential Perception

Sequential Semantic Segmentation (SE3), streaming video understanding, computation reuse, and memory- and latency-efficient inference with CNN and Transformer architectures.

Trustworthy Multimodal Perception

Vision-language model reliability, semantic alignment, uncertainty, and robustness when visual evidence is degraded, incomplete, or ambiguous.

Perception for Autonomous Systems

Scene understanding, semantic segmentation, road representation, and deployable perception for autonomous vehicles, robotics, and edge platforms.
