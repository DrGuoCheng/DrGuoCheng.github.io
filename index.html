👋 I am an Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi, where I direct the FAST Vision Lab. Before joining TAMU-CC, I worked as a software engineer at Bloomberg L.P., New York.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Purdue University in 2022, advised by Professor Jiangyu Zheng. My research develops efficient and trustworthy perception for autonomous and intelligent systems, with emphasis on streaming visual data, video scene understanding, multimodal perception, and practical deployment under constraints on computation, memory, latency, and reliability.

A signature direction of my work is Sequential Semantic Segmentation (SE3). SE3 is an inference paradigm that processes spatial or temporal inputs sequentially and reuses computation to reduce redundant processing while preserving prediction quality. I am extending this direction from large-image segmentation toward streaming video, modern neural architectures, and real-time autonomous-system applications.

My work has appeared in IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles, the International Conference on Pattern Recognition, the IEEE Intelligent Vehicles Symposium, and the IEEE International Conference on Intelligent Transportation Systems.

Current research interests include:

  • Sequential and streaming visual perception
  • Efficient, lightweight, and deployable AI
  • Trustworthy visual and multimodal intelligence
  • Autonomous vehicles, robotics, and edge systems

Semantic Misalignment in Vision-Language Models under Perceptual Degradation

Guo Cheng

arXiv preprint, 2026

PaperPDF
Abstract

Vision-language models are increasingly used in autonomous driving and embodied AI, but their behavior under realistic visual-perception degradation remains insufficiently understood. This work studies semantic misalignment using controlled degradation of semantic segmentation on Cityscapes. It introduces language-level measures of hallucination, critical omission, and safety misinterpretation, revealing a disconnect between pixel-level robustness and downstream multimodal reliability.
